## 2. $4.25 is one sale

Google Play's service fee for a developer enrolled in the 15% tier is 15% of the first $1M/yr
([Play Console Help, "Service fees"](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/112622);
[enrollment article](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10632485)).

$4.99 × 0.85 = **$4.24** `[derived]`. The reported $4.25 is one Ember Forge purchase after fee
(cent-level difference = regional price/tax/FX rounding, which Console applies per order).
Inference to confirm in Console → Financial reports → Orders: **lifetime unit sales = 1.**

So the "revenue problem" is not a pricing or conversion anomaly hiding in the ratio. It is:
**one buyer, because there were only 38 people to buy.**

## 3. Are the ratios abnormal? No — they are at or above the published norms

### 3a. Paid conversion: 1 / 38 = 2.6% `[derived]`

Primary comparables with the *same model* (free download, one-time unlock, no ads):

- **Card Crawl for Android** (Tinytouchtales, Aug 2015 launch week, Google Play "New Games"
  feature slot #32): 54,645 downloads → $8,415 through a $2.99 unlock = **4.72%**, and the
  developer states "the general average of converting a free to a paying player is **2%–3%**."
  — [tinytouchtales.com, "First week of Card Crawl for Android"](http://www.tinytouchtales.com/first-week-of-card-crawl-for-android/)
- **Shattered Pixel Dungeon** (free, optional supporter IAP): "roughly 5 million combined
  downloads and roughly 150 thousand combined sales" → **~3%** across all platforms
  `[derived]` — [shatteredpixel.com, "Ten Years of Shattered Pixel Dungeon"](https://shatteredpixel.com/blog/ten-years-of-shattered-pixel-dungeon.html)
  (Android share not broken out; ceiling, not a like-for-like).
- **Asterogue** (paid Android roguelike, solo dev, 2020): 30 Android copies in month one;
  "The Google Play store … says the conversion rate is about 1%." —
  [chr15m.itch.io devlog](https://chr15m.itch.io/asterogue/devlog/201756/sales-numbers-for-asterogue)
- **Erick Zanardo's paid puzzler** (Android, Aug 2025): 495 listing visitors → 13 direct buys
  = 2.63%; 23 orders, 8 refunds, ~US$23 gross in month one. —
  [dev.to](https://dev.to/erickzanardo/one-month-after-my-game-release-android-edition-3ncn)

Industry F2P benchmark (secondary, IAP-heavy games, mostly not our model): "install-to-payer"
is the metric Moloco tracks across 100 top publishers; they report a 6% *relative* improvement
year over year, not an absolute rate — [Moloco 2025 State of Mobile Gaming (PDF)](https://www.moloco.cloud/hubfs/Gaming%20Trends/Moloco%202025%20State%20of%20Mobile%20Gaming.pdf).
Trade blogs quoting 1.5–3.5% payer rates (e.g. earnifyhub.com) have no published methodology
and are **not** relied on here.

**Reading:** 2.6% on n=38 has a 95% Wilson interval of 0.5%–13.5% `[derived]` — it is
statistically indistinguishable from Card Crawl's 4.7% and from the 2–3% norm. Nothing in the
ratio says the Forge is mispriced or badly placed *yet*. It says the sample is 38.

### 3b. Rating rate: 2 / 38 = 5.3% `[derived]`

- Card Crawl Android, week one: 3,153 ratings / 54,645 downloads = **5.8%** `[derived]` from
  the same Tinytouchtales post (and that was with a featuring slot and a 4.12 average).
- The base rate for the whole store is far worse: an Aug-2026 crawl of 7,501 Play listings
  found only 12.2% of apps display any star rating at all
  ([testerscommunity.com](https://www.testerscommunity.com/blog/mobile-app-trends-2026)) —
  secondary, but the direction is unambiguous: most apps never get *enough* ratings to show one.

**Reading:** 5.3% is a normal, even healthy, rating rate for this model. The 5.00 average is
the best possible outcome per rating.

## 4. So what is the actual problem the ratio exposes?

Three things, in order of weight:

1. **Volume.** 38 installs in ~5 weeks of production (0.34.0 → 0.59.0, Aug 2026) is the entire
   cause of both small numbers. Every ratio is fine; the numerator is small because the
   denominator is tiny. This is a *traffic* problem (docs/research/traffic-channels.md already
   ranks channels; posting remains the owner's call).

2. **Below the display threshold.** Two ratings do not surface a star on the listing (observed
   2026-08-31, PR #98). Google does not publish the public-display minimum; the closest
   primary statement is that Console's *Total ratings* "is only available for apps that have
   received five or more ratings"
   ([Play Console Help 138230](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/138230)),
   and Play's comment policy says ratings are computed from *recent* ratings "unless the app
   has very few ratings" ([play.google comment-posting-policy](https://play.google/comment-posting-policy/)).
   Inference: the listing will show a star somewhere at/after the fifth rating. **At 5.3%
   rating rate that needs ~95 installs `[derived]`** — i.e. this fixes itself with volume, or
   sooner if the in-app ask (fixed in PR #98) fires for players who never win.
   Every listing visitor until then sees an unrated app, and cross-store survey evidence puts
   4.0★ as the consideration cliff (Apptentive 2015 survey via
   [screenfast.app synthesis](https://screenfast.app/blog/app-rating-impact-on-downloads) —
   stated intent, directional only).

3. **The ask was unreachable in the build people have.** In v0.59.0 the in-app review ask
   fires only after a *second* run win or a won daily/weekly
   (`git show v0.59.0:lib/meta/review_service.dart`, `eligible()`: `(wonThisRun && meta.runsWon >= 2) || wonDailyOrWeekly`).
   PR #98 (merged 2026-08-31, in v0.178.0+) adds the Sparktender rank-climb trigger — but
   **Play production is still 0.59.0**, so no installer has the fix. Also note the API
   contract: Play throttles the dialog per user per period and the app is never told whether
   it showed ([In-App Review guide, Quotas](https://developer.android.com/guide/playcore/in-app-review));
   it works only for accounts that installed from Play and have not already reviewed
   ([Test in-app reviews](https://developer.android.com/guide/playcore/in-app-review/test)).
   Testers' ratings from open/closed tracks never touch the public rating
   ([Set up a test, "Reviews"](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9845334)) —
   consistent with the 31-Aug five-star arriving by Console e-mail and not on the listing.

## 5. What this does NOT say

- It does not say lower the price. n=1 sale cannot distinguish $2.99 from $4.99. Card Crawl's
  developer-stated norm (2–3%) was at $2.99; our 2.6% is at $4.99 — no evidence of price
  resistance at this n.
- It does not say the ratio is "the whole business problem" in the sense of a broken funnel.
  The ratios are normal; the business problem is 38.
- It does not license adding prompts, ads, analytics, or nags (standing rule).

## 6. Owner checks that would turn inference into fact (10 minutes, Console)

## 7. Addendum (2026-09-02) — the public page says "50+", the directive says 38

The live listing's download badge reads **50+** (unauthenticated fetch, R5 §Live check). The
directive's denominator is 38. These are different metrics and the difference matters for
every ratio above:

- The badge is Play's own lifetime unique-installer bracket. **No primary Google source
  documents the bracket edges**; the widely repeated table (50+ = 51–100) comes from
  developer forums ([Stack Overflow 11487470](https://stackoverflow.com/questions/11487470),
  [56443107](https://stackoverflow.com/questions/56443107)), where Google support is quoted
  as saying the badge is based on "Total User Installs" and that "install metrics may be
  discounted". **Secondary source — treat as an inference.**
- If the bracket is right, lifetime unique installers are ≥ 51, and 38 is most likely a
  28-day or "installed audience"/active-device figure. Then the honest ratios are
  **≤ 2/51 = 3.9 % ratings** (still inside Card Crawl's 5.8 % and Shattered's ~3 % band) and
  **≤ 1/51 ≈ 2 % paid** (below Card Crawl's 4.7 % launch week, above Asterogue's ~1 %).
- Either way §3's conclusion survives: the ratios are normal for the model; the volume is the
  problem. But the paid ratio moves from "at norm" to "at the low end of norm", which puts a
  little more weight on §4.3 (the Forge is invisible on the listing) and R3 addendum 2's
  sentence-one paste.

Owner check added to owner-console-checklist.md: Statistics → **New users acquired, Lifetime**
(not 28 days) — that is the number the badge is built from, and the correct denominator.
